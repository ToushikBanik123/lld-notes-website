🎯 What Are Design Patterns?

Design patterns are standard, time-tested solutions to common software design problems. They are not code templates that you can just copy-paste, but rather abstract blueprints that guide how you structure your classes and objects.

[!example] Real-World Analogy 🍰 Think of design patterns like recipes in cooking. If you want to bake a cake, you don't experiment from scratch every time — you follow a proven recipe. Similarly, design patterns are tried-and-tested "recipes" for solving recurring coding problems efficiently.

📖 Origin: The Gang of Four (GoF)

The concept was formalized by the Gang of Four (GoF) (Erich Gamma, Richard Helm, Ralph Johnson, John Vlissides) in their famous 1994 book: "Design Patterns: Elements of Reusable Object-Oriented Software".

They cataloged 23 design patterns and grouped them into three major categories based on their purpose.

1. Creational Patterns (Object Creation)

These focus on how objects are created. They abstract the instantiation process, making the system independent of how its objects are created, composed, and represented.

  • Real-World Analogy: A vending machine. You press "Orange Juice" and get a drink. You don't care if it poured from a bottle or mixed a concentrate. The creation is hidden from you.
  • Key Patterns: Singleton, Factory Method, Abstract Factory, Builder, Prototype.

2. Structural Patterns (Object Composition)

These deal with how classes and objects are composed to form larger structures, ensuring that different pieces of a system can work together (even if they have incompatible interfaces).

  • Real-World Analogy: A travel power adapter. Your laptop charger doesn't fit the wall socket, so you use an adapter to connect them without modifying either device.
  • Key Patterns: Adapter, Decorator, Facade, Proxy.

3. Behavioral Patterns (Object Interaction)

These are concerned with how objects communicate and assign responsibilities, ensuring loose coupling between them.

  • Real-World Analogy: A restaurant waiter. You give your order to the waiter, who passes it to the chef. You don't talk to the chef directly. The waiter acts as a mediator.
  • Key Patterns: Observer, Strategy, Command, State.

🔑 Why Should You Learn Them?

  1. Avoid Reinventing the Wheel: Solve common problems using proven solutions.
  2. Common Vocabulary: Saying "I used a Strategy Pattern here" instantly explains your design to other developers.
  3. Better LLD Interviews: Patterns are heavily tested in System Design interviews.

[!quote] Key Takeaway Design patterns are the "recipes" of software engineering. Understanding the three categories — Creational (Creation), Structural (Composition), and Behavioral (Communication) — is your roadmap to mastering LLD.
