🎯 What Are SOLID Principles?

SOLID is a set of five principles for writing clean, scalable, and maintainable object-oriented code. Coined by Robert C. Martin (Uncle Bob), these principles are the backbone of good LLD.

📊 Quick Reference

PrincipleCore IdeaOne-Liner
S — [[04 - SOLID Principles/single-responsibility|SRP]]One class, one job"A class should have only one reason to change"
O — [[04 - SOLID Principles/open-closed|OCP]]Extend, don't modify"Open for extension, closed for modification"
L — [[04 - SOLID Principles/liskov-substitution|LSP]]Subclasses must be substitutable"Child should be usable wherever parent is expected"
I — [[04 - SOLID Principles/interface-segregation|ISP]]Small, focused interfaces"No client should depend on methods it doesn't use"
D — [[04 - SOLID Principles/dependency-inversion|DIP]]Depend on abstractions"High-level modules shouldn't depend on low-level ones"

🏗️ Why SOLID Matters

[!important] For Interviews Nearly every LLD interview evaluates whether you apply SOLID principles. It's not about memorizing definitions — it's about demonstrating them in your designs.

Without SOLIDWith SOLID
God classes with 1000+ linesSmall, focused classes
Changing one feature breaks three othersChanges are isolated
Can't add features without rewritingExtend with new classes
Tight coupling everywhereLoose coupling via interfaces
Untestable codeEasy to mock and unit test
