🎯 What Are Behavioral Patterns?

While Creational patterns deal with object creation and Structural patterns deal with object composition, Behavioral patterns are focused on communication and assignment of responsibilities between objects.

They help define the flow of control in a system, ensuring that objects collaborate efficiently while remaining loosely coupled.

🧩 The 10 Behavioral Patterns

There are 10 main behavioral design patterns cataloged by the Gang of Four (GoF) that we will cover:

  1. Chain of Responsibility: Passes a request along a chain of handlers.
  2. Command: Encapsulates a request as an object, allowing for undo/redo and queuing.
  3. Iterator: Provides a way to access elements of a collection sequentially without exposing its internal structure.
  4. Mediator: Reduces chaotic dependencies between objects by forcing them to communicate via a central mediator object.
  5. Memento: Captures and restores an object's internal state without violating encapsulation (Undo mechanisms).
  6. Observer: Defines a subscription mechanism to notify multiple objects about any events that happen to the object they're observing.
  7. State: Allows an object to alter its behavior when its internal state changes.
  8. Strategy: Defines a family of algorithms, encapsulates each one, and makes them interchangeable at runtime.
  9. Template Method: Defines the skeleton of an algorithm in a superclass but lets subclasses override specific steps.
  10. Visitor: Lets you separate algorithms from the objects on which they operate.

[!quote] Key Takeaway Behavioral patterns shift your focus away from control flow to focus solely on the way objects are interconnected. They are the key to building scalable, event-driven, and highly decoupled systems.
