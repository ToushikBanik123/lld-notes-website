Overview

Continuing our deep dive into classic low-level design interview problems, Part 2 focuses on heavily state-driven systems and complex entity orchestration. These problems test your ability to model real-world business constraints securely using Design Patterns like the State Pattern, Strategy Pattern, and Observer Pattern.

📝 Problems Covered

1. [[14 - Interview Problems Part 2/vending-machine|Vending Machine]]

  • Key Concepts: State Pattern (IDLE → PROCESSING_PAYMENT → DISPENSING), Inventory Management, Cash Calculation.
  • Why it’s asked: Tests your ability to handle strictly sequential workflows, concurrent access to inventory, and rollback mechanisms on failure.

2. [[14 - Interview Problems Part 2/atm-machine|ATM Machine]]

  • Key Concepts: State Pattern (Idle → CardInserted → Authenticated → TransactionSelected), Hardware Simulation, Strategy Pattern (for Withdrawals/Deposits).
  • Why it’s asked: Similar to the Vending Machine, but introduces a layer of authentication, session tracking, and more complex transaction rollback requirements.

3. [[14 - Interview Problems Part 2/hotel-management-system|Hotel Management System]]

  • Key Concepts: Advanced Entity Orchestration, Availability vs Inventory tracking, Centralized State Handler, Booking Lifecycle.
  • Why it’s asked: It is the classic example of a Booking system. It tests how you manage dynamic pricing, handle held/reserved inventory before payment completion, and deal with overlapping dates.

4. [[14 - Interview Problems Part 2/pubsub-system|PubSub System]]

  • Key Concepts: Observer Pattern, Topic Management, Asynchronous Fan-Out, Connection states (Online/Offline).
  • Why it’s asked: Tests your ability to design event-driven architectures. You must balance thread safety, decouple publishers from subscribers, and handle multiple delivery channels (e.g., Email vs Realtime).

5. [[14 - Interview Problems Part 2/snake-and-ladder|Snake and Ladder Game]]

  • Key Concepts: Singleton GameManager, Game Loop, Concurrency, Entity Management.
  • Why it’s asked: Tests your ability to handle long-running game loops sequentially while managing concurrent game sessions across threads.

6. [[14 - Interview Problems Part 2/tic-tac-toe|Tic Tac Toe Game]]

  • Key Concepts: State Array Tracking, O(1) Validation Checks, 2D Grid mapping.
  • Why it’s asked: It's a classic warmup design problem. It explicitly tests if you can optimize winning conditions (O(1) lookups) rather than writing naive O(N^2) scan loops after every single move.
