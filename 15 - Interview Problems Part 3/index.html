📚 Module 15: Interview Problems Part 3

[!info] Navigation Prev: [[14 - Interview Problems Part 2/index|← Module 14: Interview Problems Part 2]]

Overview

In the final module of the LLD series, we tackle four of the most complex, highly concurrent, and domain-heavy systems asked in senior engineering interviews. These problems test your ability to combine multiple design patterns, handle distributed constraints locally (locks), manage complex entity lifecycles, and simulate real-time systems.

📝 Problems Covered

1. [[15 - Interview Problems Part 3/digital-wallet|Digital Wallet System]]

  • Key Concepts: Local lock-based concurrency, ACID compliance emulation, Two-phase callbacks.
  • Why it’s asked: Tests your ability to handle money safely. You must prove you can prevent double-spending, handle deadlocks during transfers (using sorted lock acquisition), and orchestrate payment gateways via the Strategy Pattern.

2. [[15 - Interview Problems Part 3/elevator-system|Elevator System]]

  • Key Concepts: Simulation loops, State Pattern (PreMaintenance, Moving), Strategy Pattern (NearestElevator, FCFS).
  • Why it’s asked: It's the ultimate test of algorithm vs object-oriented design. You must decouple the movement physics from the dispatch algorithm, and handle complex asynchronous real-world hardware states.

3. [[15 - Interview Problems Part 3/music-streaming|Music Streaming Platform]]

  • Key Concepts: Byte-range streaming simulation, LRU Caching, Playback Sessions.
  • Why it’s asked: Tests your understanding of media delivery, caching large blobs, and managing stateful user sessions (PlaybackSession) across multiple requests.

4. [[15 - Interview Problems Part 3/ride-booking|Ride Booking App]]

  • Key Concepts: Geospatial proximity (mocked), Ride State Machine, Async matching.
  • Why it’s asked: Uber/Lyft designs are incredibly common. It tests how well you handle a state machine that can be mutated concurrently by two different actors (Rider cancelling vs Driver accepting).

5. [[15 - Interview Problems Part 3/movie-ticket-booking-system|Movie Ticket Booking System (BookMyShow)]]

  • Key Concepts: Concurrency Locks, Distributed Mutexes, Seat Locking.
  • Why it’s asked: The quintessential concurrency problem. It rigorously tests if you know how to safely manage inventory to prevent double-booking using locks and timeout-based reversions.
